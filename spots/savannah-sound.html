Savannah Sound

Eleuthera, Bahamas · Eleuthera · Bonefish

Read the water

A sheltered sound of white sand and grass where schools flood in from the cuts. Rental car, breakfast at the settlement, tails by nine.

The cast

Post up where the sand channels meet the grass and let the tide bring them. Schools first, then the bigger singles trailing behind, always check behind the school.

Rig

8wt, 10 to 12ft leader, tan and pink #4 to #6, olive Bitters over the grass.

Prime time

Incoming through midday sun, November through June.

When it blows out

The sound is protected from most wind. A hard norther means the south end flats near the bridge, in the lee of the island.

Bait

Shrimp, crabs, and worms across the turtle grass, glass minnows against the beach edges.

Access (DIY friendly)

Roadside pulloffs on the Queens Highway put you on the sound flats. The most famous DIY water in the Bahamas, permit required, tread lightly and release everything.

Wading (Easy)

Hard sand and firm grass through most of the sound. The middle channels get deep, plan around the flood.

Regulations change: Bahamas flats fishing regulations (license required, bones are C&R).
Since 2017 the Bahamas requires a flats fishing permit for everyone, and bonefish are catch and release. DIY wading is legal on most flats but some are guide-only by local agreement. Ask at the lodge or settlement before you walk somebody's home water, it buys you more than goodwill.
