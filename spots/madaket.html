FLYGUIDE

Madaket & Eel Point

Nantucket MA · Cape & Islands · Striped Bass, False Albacore

Read the water

Nantucket's wild west end. A ripping tide gap, bars that reshape every winter, bass in the sweep all season, and the Bonito Bar earning its name from August on.

The cast

Fish the rip seams like moving water: up-current cast, swing, step. When bonito and albies show on the bar, small translucent flies and a two-hand strip, do not lift the rod on the eat.

Rig

9 or 10wt, intermediate, sand eel flies for bass, epoxy anchovies #2 to #4 for the hardtails, real backing.

Prime time

June for bass in the rip, mid-August through September for bonito and albies, moving tide at dawn.

When it blows out

Southwest wind stacks it ugly. Great Point on the other corner of the island usually offers the lee.

Bait

Sand eels in the rip, silversides on the flats inside, bay anchovies when the hardtails arrive in August.

Access (Public)

Bike path or NRTA shuttle to Madaket, walk the beach north to Eel Point. The famous Bonito Bar sits off the point, in range on the right tides.

Wading (Easy sand, hard sweep)

Easy sand the whole way, but the Madaket rip between Smith Point and Tuckernuck moves like a river. Knee deep is the limit, the fish are inside it anyway.

Regulations change: NH Fish & Game saltwater · MA DMF recreational regs · RI DEM marine fisheries · NY DEC saltwater · NJ saltwater registry & regs · NC DMF regs (albies).
Tideline law varies hard up here. In MA and ME the intertidal zone is private to the low water mark, but fishing and fowling access is protected under the colonial ordinance. NH, RI, CT, NY, NJ: public trust below mean high water. When in doubt, stay wet.
