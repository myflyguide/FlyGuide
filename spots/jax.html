FLYGUIDE

Jacksonville Marshes

Jacksonville FL · Northeast FL · Redfish

Read the water

The flood tide tailing game. On the big tides of late summer, reds follow the water into the spartina and tail on fiddler crabs with their backs out of the water.

The cast

Find a tail, plan the shot: lead it three feet, land the crab fly soft, and move it only when the fish lifts its head. The take is visual and it will ruin you for everything else.

Rig

8wt floating, weedless crab flies (Kwan, Redfish Crack) #2 to #4, 16lb tippet, a push pole or paddle.

Prime time

The 4 to 6 biggest tide days per month, August through October, evening floods best.

When it blows out

No flood tide, no grass game. Fall back to the creek mouths on the last of the outgoing, the reds stack where bait flushes.

Bait

Fiddler crabs by the million on the flood flats, shrimp and mullet in the creeks the rest of the tide.

Access (Public ramps)

Ramps at Sisters Creek and Cedar Point put you in the spartina maze. Flood tide fishing happens on the biggest tides of the month, check the board.

Wading (Marsh mud, boat smart)

The flood tide game is played from a skiff or kayak staked at the grass edge, then careful steps on firm spots. Solo mud walking is how legends get stuck.

Regulations change: FWC saltwater regs (snook stamp, tarpon C&R, slot limits).
Florida beaches are public below the mean high water line (the wet sand). Tarpon are catch and release only, fish over 40 inches stay in the water, that is the law. Snook takes a permit stamp and has seasons and slots that change by coast, check FWC before you keep anything.
