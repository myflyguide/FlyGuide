Deadmans Cay

Long Island, Bahamas · Long Island · Bonefish

Read the water

The overlooked island. Huge, empty flats where schools of 3 to 6 pound fish work the edges and nobody else's boot prints spoil the sand.

The cast

Textbook school fishing: intercept the push, cast to the edge not the middle, and you can take multiple fish from one school if you land them away from it.

Rig

8wt, standard box, plus one heavier crab pattern for the deep edges where the big singles hold.

Prime time

November through May, incoming tide, all-day fishery with the sun.

When it blows out

West side blows out in a strong westerly, which is rare. The east side blue holes and creeks are the novelty-act fallback.

Bait

Shrimp, crabs, big worms in the marl. Schools here run bigger average than Exuma.

Access (DIY and local guides)

Vast west side flats off the settlements, walkable from side roads. Sleepy, uncrowded, and the locals will point you right if you buy lunch first.

Wading (Easy)

Miles of firm sand and marl. The distances are the challenge, pick a flat and fish it properly instead of walking to the horizon.

Regulations change: Bahamas flats fishing regulations (license required, bones are C&R).
Since 2017 the Bahamas requires a flats fishing permit for everyone, and bonefish are catch and release. DIY wading is legal on most flats but some are guide-only by local agreement. Ask at the lodge or settlement before you walk somebody's home water, it buys you more than goodwill.
