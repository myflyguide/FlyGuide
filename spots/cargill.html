Cargill Creek

Andros, Bahamas · Andros · Bonefish

Read the water

Creek-mouth bonefishing: the tide is a conveyor belt and the fish work it on schedule. Muds in the bight give blind-cast action when the light dies.

The cast

Fish the creek mouths on the last of the falling as bait flushes out, then follow the flood onto the flats. On muds, cast to the up-tide edge and count the fly down.

Rig

8wt, Gotchas and Bitters #4 to #6, one rod rigged heavy for the muds.

Prime time

October through June, tide change windows, morning light.

When it blows out

The bights bend around every wind. There is always a lee flat on Andros, that is why the lodges are here.

Bait

Shrimp and crabs flushing the creeks on the falling tide, schools mudding in the bight on the flood.

Access (Lodge hub, some DIY)

The lodge row of Andros. Walk-in flats near the creek mouths and behind the settlement, endless guided water in the bights.

Wading (Easy near the creeks)

Firm sand near the creek mouths, softer as you push into the bight. Test every step past the white sand.

Regulations change: Bahamas flats fishing regulations (license required, bones are C&R).
Since 2017 the Bahamas requires a flats fishing permit for everyone, and bonefish are catch and release. DIY wading is legal on most flats but some are guide-only by local agreement. Ask at the lodge or settlement before you walk somebody's home water, it buys you more than goodwill.
